# ForS Works Marketing Channel Analysis

**Scope:** SEO, content, paid ads, email, partnerships, and offline promotion for a one-person shop selling printed and painted 3D models.

**Decision summary:** Prioritize visual organic content and owned email capture first; use partnerships as a low-cost trust channel; delay meaningful paid acquisition until the website has one clear product category, prices, contact/checkout flow, analytics, and proof that visitors convert. Offline activity should be selective and local.

## Preconditions

The current site is not ready for efficient acquisition. The existing website analysis found that:

- the home page hides its tagline and primary CTA at normal viewport widths;
- the About and Prices pages still describe guided outdoor experiences rather than models;
- pricing CTAs do not lead to checkout, enquiry, or an email address;
- there is no analytics/event instrumentation, metadata, or email capture.

Fix these before judging channel performance. At minimum, establish one positioning statement, product/commission pages, a working enquiry or checkout path, consent-based email capture, and events for product view, CTA click, enquiry, and purchase.

## Recommended operating order

### 1. Make the destination measurable and buyable

Use a single primary CTA: **Request a commission** or **Shop available models**. Give every campaign a UTM-tagged URL. Add a lightweight analytics setup with these events:

`view_product` → `click_primary_cta` → `submit_enquiry`/`add_to_cart` → `purchase`.

Record product price, material cost, shipping, fulfilment time, and gross margin before setting an acquisition ceiling. A useful initial ceiling is:

`allowable CAC = gross profit per first order × target share allocated to acquisition`.

Do not use the existing business-plan CAC target of $10 until actual order economics support it.

### 2. Build a visual content loop

The minimum sustainable cadence is three short posts per week and one deeper case study per month. Each build should produce:

1. a finished-product carousel or photo;
2. a 15–45 second process clip;
3. a close-up/detail or comparison post;
4. a case study or email when the piece is commercially relevant.

Use a consistent CTA and label whether the item is available, commissionable, or sold. Prioritize saves, qualified profile visits, email signups, and enquiries over follower count.

### 3. Capture and nurture demand

Offer early access to limited drops, commission openings, or painting tips—whichever matches the chosen business model. Ask only for email and consent initially. Segment later by interest: ready-made models, commissions, painting content, or wholesale.

Suggested lifecycle messages:

- welcome immediately after signup;
- process/story message after 2–3 days;
- product or commission invitation after 5–7 days;
- post-purchase care/referral request after delivery;
- occasional drop/restock email, with a clear unsubscribe link.

Avoid discounts as the default; scarcity, process access, and personalization better protect the handmade positioning.

## Experiments and pass criteria

| Experiment | Setup | Primary metric | Pass condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| Content format test | 4 weeks, equal effort across finished/process/use-case posts | Qualified CTA clicks per 1,000 views | One format produces at least 2× the median qualified-click rate, with no material quality or fulfilment issue |
| Commission landing page | One page with examples, starting price/range, lead time, FAQs, and form | Enquiry rate from qualified sessions | At least 3 qualified enquiries or a clear baseline after 100 qualified sessions |
| Email waitlist | One form and three-message welcome sequence | Signup rate and enquiry/purchase rate | At least 5% signup rate on targeted traffic, then compare downstream conversion with social traffic |
| Partner referral | 3 partners, unique QR/UTM/code, 30–45 days | Qualified enquiries or contribution margin per partner | At least one partner produces repeatable qualified demand at positive contribution margin |
| Retargeting | Small capped audience of engaged site visitors/video viewers | Cost per qualified enquiry or purchase | Beats the pre-set allowable CAC; stop if tracking or landing-page conversion is unreliable |
| Local event | One event with inventory limit and QR capture | Contribution margin plus new subscribers | Positive direct contribution or a documented pipeline of qualified follow-ups |

Use a fixed test window and change one major variable at a time. Pause tests that lack enough traffic, broken tracking, or a destination mismatch rather than declaring them failures.

## 90-day plan

**Days 1–14 — foundation:** resolve the brand/product copy mismatch; fix CTA and mobile layout; publish product/commission and contact paths; add analytics, UTM conventions, consented email form, and a simple margin sheet.

**Days 15–45 — organic validation:** run the content loop; publish two product/commission pages and two case studies; start the welcome sequence; contact 10 potential partners and pilot three.

**Days 46–75 — concentrate:** keep the best-performing content format; improve the landing page from enquiry questions; publish three more specific SEO pages; run one local event or partner activation.

**Days 76–90 — paid decision:** review channel-level sessions, qualified enquiries, conversion, gross profit, and time cost. If organic/partner traffic converts and tracking is sound, test retargeting with a strict cap. Otherwise continue fixing offer and destination rather than increasing spend.

## KPI dashboard

Track weekly by source and campaign:

- qualified sessions and product/commission page views;
- CTA click-through and enquiry completion rate;
- email signup rate, open/click rate, and unsubscribe rate;
- conversion rate, average order value, gross margin, and repeat purchase;
- CAC or cost per qualified enquiry, including partner fees and event costs;
- creator hours per content asset and contribution margin per hour.

The north-star operating measure is **contribution margin from qualified demand per maker hour**, supported by conversion rate and repeat purchase. Reach and follower growth are diagnostic metrics, not success criteria.

## Initial resource allocation

For the first 90 days, allocate effort rather than a large media budget:

- 45% website/measurement/offer readiness;
- 30% reusable visual content and email;
- 15% partnership outreach and one local test;
- 10% controlled paid retargeting only after tracking and conversion are proven.

This is intentionally conservative for a one-person operation. Rebalance toward the channel that produces qualified demand at positive contribution margin without creating an unfulfillable order backlog.
