# ForS Works sales strategy

**Scope:** first-year go-to-market for a one-person studio selling handcrafted, printed and painted 3D models. Prices below are launch hypotheses and should be recalculated from material cost, labor time, packaging, fees, and shipping before publishing.

## Positioning and offer ladder

Sell a small number of visibly finished pieces, with customization as the premium service. The buyer is a collector or hobbyist who values a distinctive object and wants confidence in quality, finish, and delivery date.

| Offer | Launch price hypothesis | What is included | Sales role |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Ready-to-ship mini | $45–75 | One finished model, simple protective packaging, domestic shipping charged separately or clearly included | Low-friction first purchase |
| Signature model | $120–220 | Larger or more detailed painted model, numbered certificate, gift-ready packaging | Core margin and portfolio offer |
| Custom commission | From $300 | Brief, one concept, one revision, printed/painted final, progress photos, milestone approval | High-value service; quote only |
| Retail / small-batch order | Quote, minimum 5 units | Repeatable colorway or approved design, wholesale terms and lead time | Selective channel expansion |

Pricing guardrails: target at least 60% gross margin after materials, marketplace/payment fees, packaging, and expected rework; price labor explicitly rather than treating it as free. Require a 50% non-refundable deposit for commissions, with the balance due before dispatch. Do not discount custom work; offer a smaller scope instead.

## Channel sequence

1. **Owned site (conversion hub):** portfolio, current availability, FAQ, shipping/returns, and a clear “Buy” or “Request a commission” action. Use a hosted checkout or payment link until order volume justifies a full store. Capture email consent at checkout and enquiry.
2. **Etsy (discovery):** list 5–8 strongest ready-to-ship pieces with search-oriented titles, dimensions, materials, processing time, and ten useful photos/video clips. Treat Etsy as an acquisition channel and move repeat customers to the owned email list where permitted.
3. **Instagram / short-form video (trust):** publish finished-piece reveals, painting/process clips, scale references, and packing proof. Every post should point to one available item or the commission form. Prioritize visual channels over LinkedIn/X for consumer conversion.
4. **Niche communities (relationship):** participate in relevant miniature, model-making, tabletop, and maker communities. Teach and answer questions first; only share a product when it directly answers a request and community rules allow it.
5. **Local retailers and creators (selective):** after 10–15 fulfilled direct orders, approach two art/toy/hobby shops and three niche creators with a small, trackable consignment or affiliate test. Avoid broad consignment before demand and packaging are repeatable.

Do not launch Amazon Handmade or subscriptions in the first test period. They add catalog, fulfillment, and support complexity before the studio knows which designs sell.

## Lead qualification

Use a short form or DM script to classify every enquiry within one business day:

- **Product:** which piece or style, quantity, destination, and desired arrival date.
- **Budget:** select a range: under $150, $150–300, $300–600, or $600+.
- **Fit:** reference image or brief, acceptable style variation, and whether the request is personal, commercial, or based on someone else’s protected IP.
- **Feasibility:** deadline, shipping country, materials, and whether the requested detail can be repeated reliably.

Accept when the brief fits the studio’s style, the budget supports the quoted scope, the deadline leaves production and shipping buffer, and the IP/commercial use is legitimate. Decline or redirect when the customer wants unpaid design exploration, an impossible deadline, prohibited/counterfeit IP use, or a price below the minimum viable quote. Record source, offer, status, quoted value, and reason won/lost in one lightweight spreadsheet or CRM.

## Proposal and fulfillment flow

1. **Acknowledge:** reply with fit, expected response time, and two or three clarifying questions.
2. **Scope:** send a one-page proposal with reference image, dimensions, finish, inclusions/exclusions, timeline, shipping estimate, price, deposit, revision count, and cancellation/IP terms.
3. **Approval:** customer confirms the written scope; collect the 50% deposit before materials or production begin.
4. **Proof:** share one concept/color checkpoint. Include one revision for custom commissions; extra changes become a paid change order.
5. **Make and inspect:** produce, photograph, and quality-check against the scope. Notify immediately if the deadline or finish changes.
6. **Balance and dispatch:** collect the balance, package with tracking and care instructions, and send the delivery confirmation.
7. **Aftercare:** request a review/photo after delivery, resolve defects promptly, and invite the customer to the next release with consent.

Standard service targets: acknowledge within 1 business day, quote within 3 business days after a complete brief, and provide a realistic lead-time range rather than a false exact date.

## First-customer acquisition plan

**Days 1–7:** choose the three launch offers; calculate true unit economics; photograph the three existing models; fix the website’s mismatched hiking copy and missing conversion path; publish an enquiry/checkout destination and a simple shipping/returns policy.

**Days 8–14:** publish the three models on the owned site and Etsy; send a personal launch note to 20 relevant warm contacts/community peers; post five process or finished-work pieces; offer a numbered first-batch incentive (limited quantity, not a permanent discount).

**Days 15–30:** contact 10 qualified niche creators or community organizers with a specific collaboration proposal; run one small content test with a trackable link; follow up every open enquiry once; review which offer, source, and message generated qualified conversations and paid orders.

The initial target is 10 paid direct orders or commissions, not maximum reach. Pause paid ads until at least three offers have converted organically and the gross-margin calculation is proven. Reinvest only in the channel with the lowest qualified-customer acquisition cost and acceptable fulfillment load.

## Operating scorecard

Track weekly: qualified enquiries by channel, enquiry-to-proposal rate, proposal-to-paid rate, average order value, gross margin by offer, production hours per order, on-time dispatch rate, refunds/rework, and repeat/referral orders. The first decision gates are 10 paid orders and 30 days of data: keep, revise, or retire each offer based on margin and fulfillment effort—not likes alone.

## Assumptions requiring validation

- ForS Works is selling physical 3D-printed and hand-painted models, not guided experiences; current site copy describes both and must be made consistent.
- The studio can produce the three existing models at repeatable quality and document dimensions/materials.
- Shipping, tax, IP/licensing, and consumer-return rules vary by destination and need owner/accountant review before launch.
